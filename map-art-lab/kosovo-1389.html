Historical sequence — 01 · Geography
Source pack / method

Geography first. Tactics qualified.

This map deliberately separates geographic evidence from battle interpretation. It does not claim to recover the exact battlefield centre, unit strengths, frontage, riverbanks in 1389 or sequence of manoeuvres.

ACADEMIC ACCOUNT / FRAGMENTARY CHRONICLES

Danuta Gibas-Krzak, “Battle of Kosovo field in 1389 – legendary battle of Balkan Middle Ages”. The article stresses that there was no separate chronicler and the event must be reconstructed from fragmentary Serbian, Bosnian, Turkish and Byzantine chronicle evidence.

HISTORIOGRAPHY / MEMORY

Muhammed Al-Arnaout, “The Battle of Kosovo 1389: From Myth Making to Ideology”. Explains how sparse early detail was later expanded by political and poetic traditions.

CONTEMPORARY AND LATER RECORDS

Oytun Ayberk Fırat, “First Battle of Kosovo According to Contemporary Serbian and Dubrovnik Sources”. Compares early Serbian materials with the later Dubrovnik/Ragusan accounts, which supply additional detail.

VISUAL COMPARISON, NOT PRIMARY EVIDENCE

Kings and Generals, “Battle of Kosovo 1389 — Serbian-Ottoman Wars” (2017). A useful cinematic reference; its formation layout is a modern reconstruction, not eyewitness cartography.

Historical retrospective battle plan in GeoPortOst / Leibniz Institute collection. Useful to compare later interpretations, not proof of precise deployments.

MODERN GEOGRAPHY / ELEVATION

Elevation is sampled from Terrarium elevation tiles. Named rivers are requested from OpenStreetMap via the Overpass API when available. If river geometry cannot be loaded, the map does not substitute an invented accurate-looking river line.

Date note: 15 June 1389 Julian is commonly preserved in chronology; the strict proleptic Gregorian conversion is 23 June. The familiar 28 June date is associated with the later Vidovdan tradition in many modern summaries.
